# Running These Dungeons

Prepare to crawl, dungeon style! This book, meant for the Ref's eyes only, presents many POIs, villages, and five dungeons in Cornath, but you can putt them in any world. You can use the Cornath map to judge travel distances and the Overland Travel section in the *The Rules* to determine how long each journey takes and what happens during them. The statistics for creatures in these dungeons can be found in *The Ref*.

## Awarding Treasures

If the PCs find a magic item they've never encountered before, they aren't away of the item's properties (though they can tell the item is magic based on how it vibrates when touched). You can give the players a generic card when they find a magic item instead of the specific item's card to keep properties hidden. If the players find a *boom* *wand*, give them the magic wand card.

## Guide Not Handcuffs

The text here presents challenges, but can't account for every way to overcome them. Don't let that get in the way of fun. If the PCs have a clever idea that isn't accounted for in the text, let them try it! If it makes sense and should just work, it does. If it seems risky, call for a test.

## Dungeon Hooks

The PCs might discover the dungeon in one of the following ways. You can choose a story hook from the following list or create your own:

- Give the players a map of the region with known dungeons, villages, and POIs on it, and allow them to gather rumors on their own and decide which they feel is best suited to the PCs. This isn't just a map the players have, it's also one that their characters have, given to them by an NPC connection or found on a corpse.
- The PCs get word from an NPC connection or friendly NPC crow about a dungeon they overheard being discussed at the local tavern.
- The PCs learn of the route to a dungeon from a merchant passing through their village.
- The PCs find a map to a dungeon left behind by a group of NPC crows who went out to the location and haven't returned.
